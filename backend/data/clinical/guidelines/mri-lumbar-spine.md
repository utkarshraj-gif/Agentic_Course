# MRI of the Lumbar Spine (Synthetic Medical Policy)

> Synthetic teaching document. Not a real payer policy and not medical advice.

## Scope

This policy covers magnetic resonance imaging (MRI) of the lumbar spine for adults with low back pain, with or without radiculopathy. It applies to outpatient requests for CPT codes 72148, 72149 and 72158.

## Criteria for approval

MRI of the lumbar spine is considered medically necessary when ANY of the following red-flag conditions is documented: suspected cauda equina syndrome (new urinary retention, saddle anesthesia, bilateral leg weakness); suspected spinal infection (fever with back pain, IV drug use, recent spinal procedure); history of cancer with new back pain; progressive or severe neurologic deficit; or suspected fracture in a patient with osteoporosis or on chronic steroids.

In the absence of red flags, MRI is medically necessary when ALL of the following are documented: low back pain or radicular pain lasting at least 6 weeks; failure of at least 6 weeks of conservative therapy, which must include physical therapy or a supervised home exercise program AND an anti-inflammatory or analgesic trial unless contraindicated; and the result will change management, for example the patient is a candidate for epidural steroid injection or surgery.

## Not medically necessary

Routine imaging for acute low back pain of less than 6 weeks without red flags. Repeat imaging within 12 months without a new injury, new neurologic finding, or planned intervention. Imaging requested only for patient reassurance.

## Documentation required

Duration of symptoms in weeks; conservative therapy type, start date and duration; neurologic examination findings; presence or absence of each red flag; and the intended change in management.

## Reviewer notes

When conservative therapy is documented for fewer than 6 weeks and no red flag is present, the request should be pended for additional information rather than denied. Any denial must be reviewed by a licensed physician reviewer.
